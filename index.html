  • •
    Acoustic Measurements: Hold your phone at chest height away from your clothing to prevent frictional rustling noise.

    GPS Accuracy: Wait 5-10 seconds outdoors for satellite triangulation before hitting Save to minimize drift.

Methodology & Critical Analysis

About EcoSense & Sensor Limitations

Academic context, hardware constraints, and citizen-science methodology for our college Environmental Studies capstone.

⚠️ Academic Prototype Notice

EcoSense is a college student-led educational and citizen-science prototype. It demonstrates participatory environmental data collection but does not replace calibrated, professional environmental monitoring equipment.

Hardware Variances & Smartphone Sensor Realities

Smartphones are consumer communication devices, not calibrated laboratory instruments. Sensor accuracy, frequency response, and software drivers vary drastically between manufacturers (Apple, Samsung, Google) and individual phone models.

1. Microphone & Decibel Accuracy

Smartphone microphones feature aggressive Automatic Gain Control (AGC) and software noise-cancellation DSP tuned for human speech (300 Hz – 3400 Hz). In environmental science, uncalibrated microphones can drift by ±5 to 12 dB SPL, particularly at low sub-bass and high ultrasonic frequencies.

2. Ambient Light Sensor Inaccessibility

Most modern browsers (Chrome, Safari) restrict the W3C Generic Sensor API by default to prevent timing attacks and keystroke fingerprinting. Furthermore, smartphone light sensors point toward the user's face rather than upward toward the sky, violating cosine-correction principles.

3. Consumer GPS Drift

Urban and campus buildings cause satellite signal reflection (multi-path distortion), reducing standard consumer GPS accuracy to ±5m – 15m. Scientific GIS surveys rely on dual-frequency RTK GPS receivers with centimeter-level precision.
